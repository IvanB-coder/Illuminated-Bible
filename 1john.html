1 John

A Letter of Assurance

Writing to believers unsettled by teachers who denied Christ's humanity, John offers plain, repeatable tests for knowing that one truly belongs to God.

God is love; and he that dwelleth in love dwelleth in God, and God in him.

What happens in 1 John

John writes as an eyewitness of "the Word of life," inviting his readers into fellowship with the Father and the Son. He confronts a group who had left the church while denying that Jesus truly came in the flesh, and answers them not with elaborate argument but with clear, circling tests: does a person walk in the light, confess sin honestly, keep God's commandments, and love fellow believers in deed and not only in word? Because God is both light and love, John insists these are not separate questions — a true knowledge of God always shows itself in righteous living and genuine love for others. The letter closes by assuring "these things" are written so that believers troubled by doubt may know, with confidence, that they have eternal life.

Why it matters

1 John has given the church some of its simplest and most repeated tests for authentic faith, distilling the gospel into the rhythm of light, love, and life. Its confidence that assurance is possible — "that ye may know that ye have eternal life" — has comforted generations of believers facing doubt or opposition.

“God is love; and he that dwelleth in love dwelleth in God, and God in him.” — 1 John 4:16
