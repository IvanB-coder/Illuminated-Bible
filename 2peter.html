2 Peter

Near the end of his life, Peter warns against false teachers and scoffers, and anchors hope in the certain, if delayed, return of Christ.

Author — The Apostle Peter
Written — c. AD 65–68
Chapters — 3
Theme — Guarding truth until Christ returns
A Final Warning Before Departure

Knowing his own death is near, Peter writes to strengthen believers' grip on the truth, warning of false teachers who will exploit the church and scoffers who will mock the promise of Christ's return.

The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.

What happens in 2 Peter

Peter urges believers to add to their faith virtue, knowledge, self-control, perseverance, godliness, brotherly kindness, and love, so that they will never be unfruitful, and he grounds his authority as an eyewitness of Christ's majesty on the mount of transfiguration and of the prophetic word made more sure. The letter's second chapter turns into a sustained, vivid warning against false teachers who will secretly introduce destructive heresies, exploiting believers with greed while promising freedom even as they themselves are slaves of corruption. Peter compares their coming judgment to the certain fate of the rebellious angels, the ancient world at the flood, and Sodom and Gomorrha, showing that God knows how to rescue the godly while reserving the unjust for judgment. In the final chapter Peter addresses scoffers who will question the promise of Christ's coming by pointing out that one day is as a thousand years to the Lord, and that the apparent delay is actually God's patience giving more people opportunity to repent before that day arrives like a thief, dissolving the present heavens and earth and giving way to new heavens and a new earth where righteousness dwells.

Why it matters

2 Peter offers one of the New Testament's strongest warnings about false teaching infiltrating the church from within, and its explanation of God's patience regarding Christ's return has given generations of believers a framework for waiting without losing hope. Its call to grow in grace and knowledge remains a clear roadmap for Christian maturity.

“But grow in grace, and in the knowledge of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ.” — 2 Peter 3:18
