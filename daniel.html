Daniel

Integrity in a Foreign Court

Taken captive to Babylon as a young man, Daniel rises to high office in two empires by interpreting dreams and visions, all while quietly refusing to bow to anything but the God of his fathers.

What happens in Daniel

Daniel and three friends are chosen for the Babylonian king's court, and from the outset Daniel resolves not to defile himself with the king's food, setting a pattern of quiet, costly faithfulness that continues throughout the book. He interprets Nebuchadnezzar's dream of a great statue representing a succession of future kingdoms, watches his friends survive a fiery furnace after refusing to worship a golden image, and later interprets the king's second dream warning of his coming humiliation. After Babylon falls to the Medes and Persians, an elderly Daniel is thrown into a den of lions for continuing to pray openly to God despite a royal decree forbidding it, and he survives untouched. The book's second half shifts to apocalyptic visions given directly to Daniel, including a vision of four beasts representing successive empires and the detailed prophecy of seventy weeks concerning the coming of a Messiah, stretching the book's horizon far beyond Daniel's own generation.

Why it matters

Daniel demonstrates that genuine faith can be lived out with integrity even inside systems of power that do not share its values, and that God's sovereignty extends over every empire, not just over Israel. Its visions became foundational to how later Jewish and Christian readers understood the end of history and the coming kingdom of God.

“And the people that do know their God shall be strong, and do exploits.” — Daniel 11:32
