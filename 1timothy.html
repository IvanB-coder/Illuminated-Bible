1 Timothy

An aging apostle entrusts a young pastor with the care of a church, giving practical instructions for leadership, worship, and guarding sound doctrine.

Author — The Apostle Paul

A Manual for Church Leadership

Writing to his protégé left to oversee the church in Ephesus, Paul gives the first of his pastoral letters, full of instruction on guarding true teaching, qualifications for leaders, and how the household of God ought to conduct itself.

This is a faithful saying, and worthy of all acceptation, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief.

What happens in 1 Timothy

Paul reminds Timothy why he left him in Ephesus, to confront false teachers obsessed with myths and genealogies, and reflects with gratitude on his own conversion as proof that Christ came to save even the chief of sinners. He urges that prayers be made for all people, including kings and those in authority, and gives instructions for orderly worship, including qualifications for overseers and deacons that emphasize character, household management, and reputation over giftedness alone. Paul warns Timothy that in later times some will depart from the faith, and instructs him to be a good minister by training himself in godliness, which holds promise for both the present life and the life to come. He gives guidance on how to treat different groups within the church, including widows, elders, and slaves, and warns sharply against the love of money as a root of all kinds of evil, famously noting that those desiring riches fall into temptation and ruin. The letter closes with Paul's charge to Timothy to fight the good fight of faith, keep the commandment without spot, and guard what has been entrusted to him against the opposing ideas of false knowledge.

Why it matters

1 Timothy has shaped how churches structure leadership and worship for two millennia, offering some of Scripture's clearest qualifications for pastors and deacons. Its warnings about money and false teaching remain as urgent today as when they were first written.

“For the love of money is the root of all evil.” — 1 Timothy 6:10
