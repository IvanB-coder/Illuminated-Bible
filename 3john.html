3 John

A Letter of Commendation

Addressed to "the well beloved Gaius," this brief letter praises faithful hospitality and names a leader whose pride has gotten in the way of it.

Beloved, follow not that which is evil, but that which is good.

What happens in 3 John

John writes to Gaius, a man he loves "in the truth," praising reports that Gaius has generously housed and provisioned traveling teachers, sending them on their way in a manner worthy of God. He contrasts this with Diotrephes, a man "who loveth to have the preeminence," who refuses to welcome these same workers, speaks against John with malicious words, and even puts out of the church those who do receive them. John promises to address this in person, and closes by commending Demetrius, whose good reputation is confirmed by everyone, including the truth itself.

Why it matters

3 John offers a rare, unguarded glimpse into the everyday friction of early church life — generosity versus control, truth versus pride — and lands on a simple exhortation that still holds: imitate what is good, not what is evil, for the one who does good is of God.

“Beloved, follow not that which is evil, but that which is good.” — 3 John 1:11
