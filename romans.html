The Gospel, Argued in Full

Writing ahead of a planned visit to a church he has never met, Paul sets out the clearest, most systematic statement of the gospel in the New Testament — why everyone needs it, how it works, and what it changes.

For I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ: for it is the power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth; to the Jew first, and also to the Greek.

What happens in Romans

Paul opens by declaring that the gospel is God's power for salvation to everyone who believes, then spends the letter's first section establishing why that gospel is needed: Gentile and Jew alike stand guilty before God, with no one righteous, no not one. Against that bleak diagnosis, Paul unfolds the solution — justification by faith, not by works of the law — using Abraham as the pattern of a man made right with God simply by believing him. From there the letter turns practical and personal: those justified by faith have peace with God, are united with Christ in his death and resurrection, and are set free from sin's reign even while they wrestle with indwelling sin, a struggle Paul describes with startling honesty before declaring there is therefore now no condemnation for those in Christ Jesus. Chapters 9 through 11 pause to wrestle with Israel's unbelief and God's faithfulness to his promises, concluding with praise for the depth of God's wisdom. The final chapters move from doctrine to daily life: believers are urged to present their bodies as a living sacrifice, to let love fulfil the law, to submit to governing authorities, and to receive one another as Christ has received them, closing with warm personal greetings to friends and fellow workers in Rome.

Why it matters

Romans has shaped more theology than almost any other book in Scripture, supplying the backbone for how Christians have understood sin, grace, faith, and the Christian life ever since. Its argument that right standing with God comes by faith rather than performance has anchored movements of renewal across church history and remains the clearest single explanation of the gospel's logic in the Bible.

“Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ.” — Romans 5:1
