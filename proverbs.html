The Fear of the Lord is the Beginning of Wisdom

Proverbs is wisdom literature distilled into short, memorable lines meant to be carried into ordinary moments — how to handle money, anger, friendship, work, and temptation. It opens with a father urging his son toward wisdom and away from folly, then gathers hundreds of pointed sayings from Solomon and other wise men, covering everything from laziness to generosity to the famous portrait of a virtuous woman that closes the book.

The fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge: but fools despise wisdom and instruction.

What happens in Proverbs

Proverbs opens by personifying wisdom and folly as two women calling out to anyone who will listen, urging the reader to choose the fear of the Lord as the starting point for a wise life. The bulk of the book, attributed mainly to Solomon, is a collection of short, two-line sayings that contrast the wise with the foolish, the diligent with the lazy, the righteous with the wicked, touching on nearly every area of daily life: how to speak, how to handle money, how to treat a neighbour, how to raise a child, how to govern desire. Later sections gather the sayings of Agur and King Lemuel, including Lemuel's description of the virtues to seek in a king and in a wife. The book closes with the well-known acrostic poem praising a woman of noble character, capable, hardworking, generous, and feared by no one because she trusts in the Lord.

Why it matters

Proverbs matters because wisdom in Scripture is never just information, it is skill for living well in a world God made orderly. These sayings refuse to separate faith from daily decisions; how a person handles their tongue, their work, their wallet, and their relationships are all treated as matters of the heart's posture toward God. For a Father who wants his children to flourish and not merely survive, Proverbs offers practical, tested wisdom, reminding every reader that true understanding starts not with cleverness but with reverence for the living God.

“Trust in the Lord with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.” — Proverbs 3:5–6
