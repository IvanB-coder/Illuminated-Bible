Ordering a Young, Unruly Church

Left behind on Crete to finish what Paul started, Titus receives practical instructions for appointing leaders and shaping a notoriously difficult culture toward godliness through grace-shaped living.

For the grace of God that bringeth salvation hath appeared to all men, teaching us that, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, we should live soberly, righteously, and godly, in this present world.

What happens in Titus

Paul instructs Titus to appoint elders in every town on Crete, giving qualifications nearly identical to those given Timothy, and warns bluntly about the island's reputation, quoting a Cretan prophet who called his own people liars, evil beasts, and lazy gluttons. He gives instructions for how older men, older women, younger men, and servants ought to live, so that the teaching about God their Savior would not be slandered, and grounds all of this ethical instruction in the grace of God that has appeared, training believers to live soberly and righteously while waiting for the blessed hope of Christ's glorious appearing. Paul reminds Titus that believers were once themselves foolish and disobedient before being saved not by righteous works but by God's mercy, through the washing of regeneration and renewing of the Holy Ghost, a salvation meant to produce believers careful to maintain good works. He instructs Titus to avoid foolish controversies and to reject a divisive person after a couple of warnings, closing with plans to send Artemas or Tychicus to relieve Titus and personal travel instructions.

Why it matters

Titus distills the relationship between grace and good works as clearly as any book in the New Testament, insisting that genuine doctrine always reshapes behavior rather than remaining merely theoretical. Its blunt realism about human nature, paired with confidence in grace's transforming power, offers a model for ministry in difficult cultural settings.

“Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us.” — Titus 3:5
