Judgment, Then Glory

Isaiah prophesies through the reigns of four kings of Judah, warning of exile for a nation that has traded justice for ritual, while promising a remnant, a Messiah, and a renewed creation on the far side of judgment.

What happens in Isaiah

Isaiah receives his call in the year King Uzziah dies, seeing the Lord high and lifted up in the temple and crying out in his own uncleanness before a burning coal touches his lips and sends him out to speak. The book's first half confronts Judah and the surrounding nations with their pride, idolatry, and empty religion, warning that the Assyrian empire will become God's instrument of discipline, even as a remnant is promised survival and a future king, described as a shoot from the stump of Jesse, who will rule in righteousness. The narrative pauses in the middle of the book to recount King Hezekiah's deliverance from Assyria's siege and his later folly in showing Babylon's envoys all his treasures, foreshadowing a future exile. From there, Isaiah's tone shifts from warning to comfort, speaking to a people not yet exiled as though their captivity in Babylon has already happened and already ended, offering words of comfort and announcing a servant of the Lord who will be despised, rejected, and pierced for the transgressions of others, carrying their sorrows so that by his wounds they might be healed. The book closes with visions of restored Jerusalem, a highway of holiness, and ultimately new heavens and a new earth where former troubles are forgotten.

Why it matters

Isaiah is quoted more often in the New Testament than almost any other Old Testament book, and its portrait of the suffering servant became the clearest Old Testament picture of what the cross would accomplish centuries before it happened. It holds together two truths that are easy to separate: God's holiness demands justice, and God's love provides a way through judgment to genuine comfort.

“But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed.” — Isaiah 53:5
